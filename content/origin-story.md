A project originally borne of necessity soon uncovered a passion for the project; during this four-year period in the early 2010s, it was serendipity that brought Robin to the traditional Latin Mass at St. Lawrence Martyr Roman-Catholic Church in Toronto's Diocesan designated parish for the Extraordinary Form.

Soon after finding the small community and the contemplative relative silence of the Missa Lecta (Low Mass), the only weekday altar server suddenly passed away leaving nobody to serve at the altar.

Having graduated from Ontario's last cohort of high school students who had been able to complete the full complement of the province's 'five-year Latin program' (which included two "Grade 13" Latin courses), I meekly volunteered to learn how to serve at the altar.

I remember the first informal training session, the classical pronunciations instinctively came out--hard "C"'s, saecula saeculorums that sounded like someone referring to an Alfred Hitchcock film--but before long, somehow, the Ecclesiastical Latin became comfortable and I then faced weeks of confusion--having only just begun attending the Extraordinary Form--I had still no concept of "what's going on here?" having only learned the Novus Ordo as an adult convert a decade before--and the near silence of the Missa Lecta didn't give much indication of what was going on, either.

This 'Mass panic' about feeling totally lost despite having been a full-blown Catholic for a decade already by then, is perhaps the number one challenge I hope to help people who are intimidated by the language and complexity preventing them from participating in the Holy Mass as it had been for over a millenium as well as for regular attendees who had been brought up in the traditional liturgy--but may never have felt understanding it to be accessible:

To those ends, I have designed this app and its adjunct educational and social materials and forum to facilitate developing an understanding of the Latin Mass and its prayers:

 - the Mass's 'backbone' will be easily recognizable to modern Catholics in the form of a simplified "Subway Map"
    - like a Christmas Tree, the "Subway Map" representation is a straight line backbone with set points along it: the Confiteor, the Kyrie, the Gloria adorn the Tree in the same relative places as in the modern "Novus Ordo" Mass
